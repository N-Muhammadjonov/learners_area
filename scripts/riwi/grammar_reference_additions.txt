1|An ordinary statement normally places its subject before its finite verb; objects and complements follow the appropriate verb pattern.|We study English.~Biz ingliz tilini o‘rganamiz.|The room feels warm.~Xona iliq tuyulyapti.
2|Word class depends on how a word functions in its sentence, not just its spelling.|The train is fast.~Poyezd tez yuradi.|The train moves fast.~Poyezd tez harakatlanadi.
3|A full independent clause normally needs a subject and a finite verb; an imperative can leave its subject understood.|My brother works.~Akam ishlaydi.|Please wait here.~Iltimos, shu yerda kuting.
4|Present forms of be connect the subject to an identity, state or place; match am, is and are to the subject.|She is a teacher.~U o‘qituvchi.|We are at home.~Biz uydamiz.
5|Put not after be for a negative, and put be before the subject for an ordinary yes/no question.|They are not ready.~Ular tayyor emas.|Are you tired?~Charchadingizmi?
6|Subject, object, possessive determiner and possessive pronoun forms occupy different positions.|She gave it to me.~U buni menga berdi.|This pen is hers.~Bu ruchka uniki.
7|Countable nouns can be singular or plural; regular endings and irregular plural forms must be distinguished.|Three women are waiting.~Uch ayol kutyapti.|These boxes are heavy.~Bu qutilar og‘ir.
8|Choose a or an by the first pronounced sound of a singular countable expression.|He is a university student.~U universitet talabasi.|We waited for an hour.~Bir soat kutdik.
9|The commonly identifies a referent the listener can locate in context; it is not required before every noun.|Please close the door.~Iltimos, eshikni yoping.|I bought a book. The book was useful.~Kitob oldim. O‘sha kitob foydali ekan.
10|Demonstratives distinguish singular/plural and contextually nearer/more distant referents.|This chair is comfortable.~Bu stul qulay.|Those houses are old.~Anavi uylar eski.
11|For present possession, use has with third-person singular subjects and have with the other ordinary subjects.|We have enough time.~Vaqtimiz yetarli.|The house has two doors.~Uyning ikkita eshigi bor.
12|Existential there introduces something present or existing; standard agreement follows the introduced noun phrase.|There is a park nearby.~Yaqin atrofda bog‘ bor.|There are two messages.~Ikkita xabar bor.
13|A wh-word identifies the information sought; the remaining question still follows its appropriate auxiliary pattern.|When does the lesson start?~Dars qachon boshlanadi?|Why are you waiting?~Nega kutyapsiz?
14|And adds, but contrasts, and or offers alternatives; coordinate elements with compatible grammatical roles.|She reads and writes.~U o‘qiydi va yozadi.|Would you like tea or water?~Choy ichasizmi yoki suv?
15|In, on and at have common place/time patterns, but individual combinations also need to be learned.|The keys are on the table.~Kalitlar stol ustida.|The class starts at nine.~Dars soat to‘qqizda boshlanadi.
16|In ordinary present negatives without another auxiliary, use do/does not and the base verb.|I do not drive.~Men mashina haydamayman.|She does not work here.~U bu yerda ishlamaydi.
17|Capitalise sentence beginnings, I and appropriate proper names; punctuation marks the intended sentence type.|My name is Lola.~Mening ismim Lola.|Is Monday convenient?~Dushanba sizga qulaymi?
18|Present simple commonly describes habits, schedules and general states; third-person singular adds its appropriate ending.|He walks to work.~U ishga piyoda boradi.|Water freezes at zero degrees Celsius under ordinary pressure.~Odatdagi bosimda suv Selsiy bo‘yicha nol darajada muzlaydi.
19|Do/does carries the question agreement, leaving the main verb in its base form.|Do they study here?~Ular shu yerda o‘qiydimi?|Where does she work?~U qayerda ishlaydi?
20|Present continuous combines present be with an -ing form for an ongoing or temporary activity.|She is writing a letter.~U xat yozyapti.|We are staying with friends this week.~Bu hafta do‘stlarimiznikida turibmiz.
21|Choose the simple or progressive according to the intended habitual, general, ongoing or temporary meaning.|She usually walks, but today she is taking the bus.~U odatda piyoda yuradi, bugun esa avtobusda ketyapti.|I read every evening. I am reading now.~Har oqshom o‘qiyman. Hozir ham o‘qiyapman.
22|Regular past forms commonly end in -ed; did already marks the past in questions and negatives.|She cleaned her room yesterday.~U kecha xonasini tozaladi.|Did you finish the task?~Vazifani tugatdingizmi?
23|Irregular past forms are lexical patterns to learn, not forms made by adding -ed to every verb.|I bought some bread.~Non sotib oldim.|They saw a bird.~Ular qushni ko‘rdi.
24|Use was/were for past be, matching the subject; do not add did to an ordinary be question.|I was at home.~Uyda edim.|Were you busy yesterday?~Kecha bandmidingiz?
25|Will plus a base verb can express decisions, predictions or promises; context determines the meaning.|I will help you.~Sizga yordam beraman.|It will be cold tonight.~Bugun kechasi sovuq bo‘ladi.
26|Be going to commonly presents an intention or a prediction grounded in visible evidence.|We are going to study together.~Birga o‘qimoqchimiz.|Look at the clouds. It is going to rain.~Bulutlarga qarang. Yomg‘ir yog‘adiganga o‘xshaydi.
27|Can takes a base verb without third-person -s and can express ability, permission or possibility.|Can I sit here?~Shu yerga o‘tirsam bo‘ladimi?|He cannot drive.~U mashina hayday olmaydi.
28|An imperative usually uses a base verb with an understood you; do not makes an ordinary negative imperative.|Turn left at the corner.~Burilishda chapga buriling.|Do not touch the screen.~Ekranga tegmang.
29|Countability depends on the noun's sense; mass nouns commonly need a measure expression for individual units.|There are three chairs.~Uchta stul bor.|Could I have a glass of water?~Bir stakan suv olsam bo‘ladimi?
30|Some and any vary with polarity, expectation and communicative purpose, including offers and requests.|We have some apples.~Olmalarimiz bor.|Would you like some water?~Suv ichasizmi?
31|Many combines with plural count nouns, much with mass nouns, and a lot of can combine with either.|There are many reasons.~Sabablar ko‘p.|We have a lot of work.~Ishimiz ko‘p.
32|An adjective commonly comes before a noun or after a linking verb, rather than simply following its noun.|She has a red bag.~Uning qizil sumkasi bor.|The bag is red.~Sumka qizil.
33|Frequency adverbs commonly precede lexical verbs but follow be; focus and context permit variation.|I often read at night.~Kechasi tez-tez o‘qiyman.|He is always polite.~U doim muloyim.
34|Place and time adjuncts often appear near the end, with place before time, but discourse can change the order.|They met in the park on Sunday.~Ular yakshanba kuni bog‘da uchrashdi.|Yesterday we worked at home.~Kecha uyda ishladik.
35|Object pronouns commonly follow verbs or prepositions; they do not replace subject pronouns indiscriminately.|She called him.~U unga qo‘ng‘iroq qildi.|This gift is for them.~Bu sovg‘a ular uchun.
36|Possessive apostrophes differ between ordinary singular nouns, regular plurals and irregular plurals.|The teacher's desk is new.~O‘qituvchining stoli yangi.|The students' bags are outside.~O‘quvchilarning sumkalari tashqarida.
37|When who/what is the subject, ordinary do-support is absent; object questions have a different structure.|Who called you?~Sizga kim qo‘ng‘iroq qildi?|Who did you call?~Siz kimga qo‘ng‘iroq qildingiz?
38|Use a suitable comparative form, with than when the comparison explicitly names its second term.|This bag is lighter than mine.~Bu sumka menikidan yengilroq.|The new method is more reliable.~Yangi usul ishonchliroq.
39|Superlatives identify an extreme within a relevant group; irregular forms need separate learning.|This is the smallest room.~Bu eng kichik xona.|She chose the most practical option.~U eng amaliy variantni tanladi.
40|Movement prepositions distinguish destination, entry, movement onto a surface and origin.|He jumped onto the platform.~U platforma ustiga sakradi.|We walked from school to the park.~Maktabdan bog‘gacha piyoda bordik.
41|Verb complementation is lexical: enjoy takes -ing, while like can allow different patterns according to meaning.|They love walking.~Ular piyoda yurishni yoqtiradi.|I like to check my work before sending it.~Ishni yuborishdan oldin tekshirishni ma’qul ko‘raman.
42|Because introduces a reason, while so can connect that reason to its result.|Because I was busy, I stayed home.~Band bo‘lganim uchun uyda qoldim.|The road was closed, so we waited.~Yo‘l yopiq edi, shuning uchun kutdik.
43|Present perfect relates an earlier situation to the present; an explicitly finished past time normally favours past simple.|She has finished the report.~U hisobotni tugatdi; hozir u tayyor.|Have you tried this dish?~Bu taomni tatib ko‘rganmisiz?
44|For measures a duration; since identifies its starting point. The wider construction determines whether the situation continues.|I have lived here since March.~Martdan beri shu yerda yashayman.|He has worked here for two years.~U ikki yildan beri shu yerda ishlaydi.
45|Already, yet, just, ever and never have different meanings and typical positions in perfect constructions.|I have just arrived.~Hozirgina yetib keldim.|She has not replied yet.~U hali javob bermadi.
46|Distinguish a finished past-time frame from experience or results connected to now.|I have visited Bukhara twice.~Buxoroga ikki marta borganman.|I visited Bukhara last summer.~O‘tgan yoz Buxoroga bordim.
47|Past continuous presents an activity in progress at a past reference point, often as background.|They were eating when I arrived.~Men kelganimda ular ovqatlanayotgan edi.|At nine, she was studying.~Soat to‘qqizda u o‘qiyotgan edi.
48|Past simple can narrate successive events, while past continuous can present background activity; while is not an automatic tense rule.|I opened the door and sat down.~Eshikni ochib o‘tirdim.|While we were talking, the lights went out.~Gaplashayotganimizda chiroq o‘chdi.
49|Present continuous can refer to an arranged future activity when context supplies the future time.|We are having lunch together tomorrow.~Ertaga birga tushlik qilamiz.|She is leaving on Friday.~U juma kuni jo‘naydi.
50|In ordinary future time clauses, a present form follows when/after/until rather than a future will form.|We will start after he arrives.~U kelganidan keyin boshlaymiz.|Wait until the light turns green.~Chiroq yashil bo‘lguncha kuting.
51|Must and have to overlap in obligation, but must not means prohibition and do not have to means absence of necessity.|You must not park here.~Bu yerga mashina qo‘yish taqiqlangan.|You do not have to come early.~Erta kelishingiz shart emas.
52|Should and ought to commonly express advice or expectation; should does not take to.|You ought to check the address.~Manzilni tekshirganingiz ma’qul.|She should arrive soon.~U tez orada yetib kelishi kerak.
53|May, might and could can express possibility; their strength and negative interpretations depend on context.|They may be at home.~Ular uyda bo‘lishi mumkin.|The answer could be different.~Javob boshqacha bo‘lishi mumkin.
54|Could commonly describes past general ability; managed to/was able to often suits one successful event.|I could swim when I was young.~Yoshligimda suza olardim.|She was able to open the door.~U eshikni ocha oldi.
55|Used to plus a base verb presents a former habit or state, commonly contrasted with the present.|He used to cycle to work.~U ilgari ishga velosipedda borardi.|We did not use to have a car.~Ilgari mashinamiz yo‘q edi.
56|Be/get used to takes a noun or -ing form because to is a preposition in this construction.|She is used to the noise.~U shovqinga ko‘nikkan.|I am getting used to working early.~Erta ishlashga ko‘nikib boryapman.
57|Choose -ing or an infinitive according to the governing verb and intended meaning, not one universal translation rule.|We avoided making a mistake.~Xato qilishdan saqlandik.|He hopes to return soon.~U tez orada qaytishga umid qiladi.
58|A realistic future condition commonly combines an if-present clause with a modal result clause.|If she calls, I will answer.~U qo‘ng‘iroq qilsa javob beraman.|If we leave now, we can catch the bus.~Hozir chiqsak, avtobusga ulguramiz.
59|A zero conditional can express a general relationship or a regularly repeated consequence, not just scientific laws.|If I feel tired, I take a break.~Charchasam, tanaffus qilaman.|When the button is pressed, the light comes on.~Tugma bosilganda chiroq yonadi.
60|A past form in a second conditional can mark hypothetical distance rather than past time.|If I were you, I would wait.~O‘rningizda bo‘lsam kutardim.|If she knew the answer, she would tell us.~Javobni bilsa, bizga aytardi.
61|A defining relative identifies which person or thing is meant; its subject pronoun cannot simply be omitted.|The woman who teaches us is kind.~Bizga dars beradigan ayol mehribon.|The phone that rang is mine.~Jiringlagan telefon meniki.
62|An object relative pronoun can be omitted in a defining clause; omitting a subject relative changes or breaks the structure.|The book I borrowed is useful.~Olib turgan kitobim foydali.|The person you called has arrived.~Siz qo‘ng‘iroq qilgan odam keldi.
63|Too expresses excess, while enough expresses sufficiency and follows an adjective but precedes a noun.|The bag is too heavy to carry.~Sumka ko‘tarish uchun haddan tashqari og‘ir.|We have enough chairs.~Stullarimiz yetarli.
64|So modifies an adjective/adverb; such introduces a noun phrase, often with an article for a singular count noun.|The room was so quiet.~Xona juda sokin edi.|It was such a useful lesson.~Bu juda foydali dars bo‘ldi.
65|Comparatives take suitable degree modifiers such as much, far or slightly rather than an extra comparative ending.|This route is slightly longer.~Bu yo‘l sal uzunroq.|Her answer is much clearer.~Uning javobi ancha tushunarliroq.
66|Reflexive pronouns can identify a subject acting on itself or add emphasis; they are not ordinary subject-pronoun substitutes.|She introduced herself.~U o‘zini tanishtirdi.|We repaired it ourselves.~Uni o‘zimiz tuzatdik.
67|Each/every commonly takes a singular count noun; all and both select different number and grouping patterns.|Each student has a book.~Har bir o‘quvchida kitob bor.|All the students are ready.~O‘quvchilarning hammasi tayyor.
68|Either selects one of two possibilities; neither excludes both. Agreement can vary with construction and register.|You can choose either seat.~Ikki o‘rindiqdan xohlaganingizni tanlashingiz mumkin.|Neither of the doors is open.~Ikkala eshik ham ochiq emas.
69|Canonical tags match the auxiliary and pronoun, commonly reversing polarity; intonation affects whether confirmation is expected.|She is ready, isn't she?~U tayyor, shundaymi?|You have finished, haven't you?~Tugatdingiz, shundaymi?
70|The embedded part of an indirect question normally has statement order, not direct-question inversion.|Could you tell me where the station is?~Bekat qayerdaligini ayta olasizmi?|I wonder why he left.~U nega ketganiga qiziqyapman.
71|Geographical names have conventional article patterns; countries, rivers and plural names do not all behave alike.|The Nile is a river.~Nil — daryo.|She lives in Uzbekistan.~U O‘zbekistonda yashaydi.
72|Present perfect continuous highlights an activity extending towards now or its present evidence; not every state takes -ing.|She has been working all morning.~U ertalabdan beri ishlayapti.|It has been raining.~Yomg‘ir yog‘ib turibdi; uning hozirgi izi yoki davomiyligi nazarda tutilgan.
73|Perfect simple often highlights completion or quantity; perfect continuous often highlights the process or duration.|I have read three chapters.~Uch bob o‘qib bo‘ldim.|I have been reading for an hour.~Bir soatdan beri o‘qiyapman.
74|Past perfect locates a situation before a past reference point; it is not obligatory for every past event.|She had eaten before we arrived.~Biz kelishimizdan oldin u ovqatlanib bo‘lgan edi.|I realised I had lost my ticket.~Chiptamni yo‘qotganimni tushundim.
75|Past perfect continuous looks back from a past point at an activity's duration or continuing effects.|We had been walking for hours before we stopped.~To‘xtashimizdan oldin soatlab yurgan edik.|He was tired because he had been working.~Ishlab kelgani uchun u charchagan edi.
76|Future continuous presents an activity in progress at a future point and can also make an enquiry less direct.|This time tomorrow, we will be travelling.~Ertaga shu paytda yo‘lda ketayotgan bo‘lamiz.|Will you be using the car tonight?~Bugun kechqurun mashinadan foydalanasizmi?
77|Future perfect places completion before a specified future deadline; by and until express different time relations.|By Friday, she will have finished.~Juma kunigacha u tugatgan bo‘ladi.|We will have left before you arrive.~Siz kelishingizdan oldin jo‘nab ketgan bo‘lamiz.
78|Future perfect continuous measures an activity's duration up to a future reference point.|By June, she will have been teaching for ten years.~Iyunga kelib uning dars berayotganiga o‘n yil bo‘ladi.|At six, we will have been waiting for an hour.~Soat oltida kutayotganimizga bir soat bo‘ladi.
79|Future in the past presents a later event from an earlier viewpoint; would can have other meanings in other contexts.|He promised he would call.~U qo‘ng‘iroq qilishga va’da berdi.|We were going to leave, but it rained.~Ketmoqchi edik, lekin yomg‘ir yog‘di.
80|A passive uses a form of be and a past participle, with its subject corresponding to an affected participant.|The room is cleaned daily.~Xona har kuni tozalanadi.|The letter was sent yesterday.~Xat kecha yuborildi.
81|Passive voice combines with tense/aspect and modals in a fixed auxiliary order; heavier forms need a real communicative purpose.|The work has been completed.~Ish tugallangan.|The door must be closed.~Eshik yopilishi kerak.
82|By commonly names a passive agent; with commonly names an instrument. The intended relationship matters.|The poem was written by a student.~She’rni bir o‘quvchi yozgan.|The paper was cut with scissors.~Qog‘oz qaychi bilan kesilgan.
83|Get-passives often foreground an event or its effect and are common in less formal contexts; they do not replace every be-passive.|My bag got damaged.~Sumkam shikastlandi.|They got invited to the event.~Ularni tadbirga taklif qilishdi.
84|Reported statements may backshift after a past reporting verb, but continuing truth and context can support an unshifted form.|He said that he was busy.~U bandligini aytdi.|She told me that she would return.~U menga qaytishini aytdi.
85|Reported questions use statement order; yes/no questions are commonly introduced by if or whether.|He asked where I lived.~U qayerda yashashimni so‘radi.|They asked if we had finished.~Ular tugatganimizni so‘radi.
86|Reported requests and commands commonly use ask/tell plus an object and an infinitive, with not before to for negatives.|She asked me to wait.~U mendan kutishni so‘radi.|He told us not to worry.~U bizga xavotir olmaslikni aytdi.
87|Reporting verbs select particular complement patterns; learn the verb and its construction together.|He admitted making a mistake.~U xato qilganini tan oldi.|She promised to help.~U yordam berishga va’da berdi.
88|A third conditional imagines a past condition and a past consequence that did not occur in that way.|If we had left earlier, we would have caught the train.~Oldinroq chiqqanimizda poyezdga ulgurgan bo‘lardik.|If she had called, I would have answered.~U qo‘ng‘iroq qilganida javob bergan bo‘lardim.
89|Mixed conditionals combine different time references; the condition and result must remain logically compatible.|If I had slept, I would feel better now.~Uxlaganimda hozir o‘zimni yaxshiroq his qilardim.|If he were more careful, he would not have lost it.~U ehtiyotkorroq bo‘lganida uni yo‘qotmagan bo‘lardi.
90|Unless commonly means if not, while provided that/as long as emphasise a required condition; check the actual logical meaning.|We will leave unless it rains.~Yomg‘ir yog‘masa jo‘naymiz.|You can stay as long as you keep quiet.~Jim o‘tirsangiz qolishingiz mumkin.
91|Wish plus a past form can express present unreality; wish plus past perfect commonly expresses past regret.|I wish I had more time.~Koshki vaqtim ko‘proq bo‘lsa.|She wishes she had studied harder.~U ko‘proq o‘qimaganidan afsuslanadi.
92|Wish/if only plus would often requests a change or expresses annoyance; it is not a general substitute for every wish.|I wish you would listen.~Koshki gapimni tinglasangiz.|If only the bus would arrive!~Avtobus tezroq kelsa edi!
93|Non-defining relatives add supplementary information with commas; they do not take that or omit their relative pronoun.|My aunt, who lives nearby, called.~Yaqinda yashaydigan xolam qo‘ng‘iroq qildi.|The bridge, which opened last year, is busy.~O‘tgan yili ochilgan ko‘prikda qatnov ko‘p.
94|Where, when and whose express place, time and possession relationships; whose can also refer to things.|This is the house where I grew up.~Bu men ulg‘aygan uy.|We visited a company whose products are exported.~Mahsulotlari eksport qilinadigan kompaniyaga bordik.
95|A relative preposition can be stranded at the end or fronted with whom/which in a more formal pattern.|The woman I spoke to was helpful.~Men gaplashgan ayol yordam berdi.|The woman to whom I spoke was helpful.~Men murojaat qilgan ayol yordam berdi; bu rasmiyroq shakl.
96|A content clause can function as subject, object or complement; whether is generally preferred to if in a subject question-clause.|I believe that she is right.~U haq deb o‘ylayman.|Whether we can finish depends on the weather.~Tugata olishimiz ob-havoga bog‘liq.
97|Adverbial clauses express relations such as time and reason; a word such as since can have more than one interpretation.|While I cooked, she read.~Men ovqat qilganimda u o‘qidi.|Because the road was closed, we waited.~Yo‘l yopiq bo‘lgani uchun kutdik.
98|Although/even though typically introduce concession, whereas contrasts situations; do not mechanically add but to the same link.|Even though she was tired, she continued.~Charchagan bo‘lsa ham u davom etdi.|I prefer tea, whereas he prefers coffee.~Men choyni ma’qul ko‘raman, u esa qahvani.
99|Despite and in spite of are prepositions taking nouns or -ing forms, unlike although which introduces a clause.|In spite of feeling tired, he worked.~Charchaganiga qaramay u ishladi.|Despite the delay, we arrived on time.~Kechikishga qaramay vaqtida yetib keldik.
100|Infinitival purpose and finite so that clauses have different structures, especially when subjects differ.|I called to ask a question.~Savol so‘rash uchun qo‘ng‘iroq qildim.|She spoke slowly so that we could understand.~Biz tushunishimiz uchun u sekin gapirdi.
101|Result constructions combine so with an adjective/adverb or such with a noun phrase, followed by that.|She was so tired that she fell asleep.~U shu qadar charchagan ediki, uxlab qoldi.|It was such a long journey that we needed a break.~Safar shunchalik uzoq ediki, tanaffus qilishimiz kerak bo‘ldi.
102|Have/get plus object and past participle can describe arranging for another person to do a service.|She had her hair cut.~U sochini kestirdi.|We got the windows cleaned.~Derazalarni tozalattirdik.
103|The governing verb determines whether an object is followed by a to-infinitive or a bare infinitive.|I want you to stay.~Qolishingizni xohlayman.|They let us enter.~Ular kirishimizga ruxsat berdi.
104|After perception verbs, -ing can foreground part of a process while a bare infinitive can present an event as a whole.|I saw her crossing the road.~Uning yo‘ldan o‘tayotganini ko‘rdim.|I saw her cross the road.~Uning yo‘lni kesib o‘tganini ko‘rdim.
105|Agreement follows the grammatical head and construction, not simply the nearest plural noun.|Everyone is ready.~Hamma tayyor.|A number of students are absent.~Bir qancha o‘quvchi kelmagan.
106|A few/a little present a small amount positively; few/little often imply less than wanted, with count/mass distinctions.|I have a few friends here.~Bu yerda bir nechta do‘stim bor.|There is little hope.~Umid juda kam.
107|Some phrasal verbs allow separation and place a pronoun between verb and particle; others do not separate.|Please pick it up.~Iltimos, uni ko‘tarib oling.|She looks after her brother.~U ukasiga qaraydi.
108|Verbs have conventional preposition and complement patterns, which should be learned with their meanings.|We depend on public transport.~Jamoat transportiga tayanamiz.|She succeeded in finding a solution.~U yechim topishga muvaffaq bo‘ldi.
109|The simple/progressive contrast concerns aspect and viewpoint; always plus progressive can also convey a speaker's attitude.|He always leaves early.~U doim erta ketadi.|He is always leaving his keys behind!~U hadeb kalitlarini unutib qoldiraveradi!
110|Some verbs have state and activity senses; progressive suitability follows the intended sense, not only the verb spelling.|I think the plan is useful.~Reja foydali deb o‘ylayman.|I am thinking about the plan.~Reja haqida o‘ylayapman.
111|A perfect construction can relate events to an unfinished current time period; a finished reference point changes the choice.|I have answered three emails today.~Bugun uchta xatga javob berdim; bugungi davr hali davom etyapti.|I answered three emails yesterday.~Kecha uchta xatga javob berdim.
112|Habitual would commonly presents repeated past actions, while used to also readily describes former states.|Every evening, she would read to us.~Har oqshom u bizga kitob o‘qib berardi.|He used to be shy.~U ilgari tortinchoq edi.
113|Be to, be due to and be about to differ in formality, scheduling and imminence.|The train is due to arrive at ten.~Poyezd soat o‘nda kelishi kutilmoqda.|The show is about to begin.~Tomosha hozir boshlanish arafasida.
114|Future probability expressions indicate different degrees of commitment, but no modal has a fixed universal percentage.|The plan is likely to succeed.~Rejaning amalga oshish ehtimoli yuqori.|It might rain later.~Keyinroq yomg‘ir yog‘ishi mumkin.
115|Epistemic must draws a strong conclusion; can't commonly expresses a strong negative deduction rather than prohibition.|They must be at home; the lights are on.~Chiroqlar yoniq, ular uyda bo‘lsa kerak.|That cannot be the right address.~Bu to‘g‘ri manzil bo‘lishi mumkin emas.
116|A modal plus have and a past participle makes a deduction about an earlier situation.|She might have missed the bus.~U avtobusga ulgurmagan bo‘lishi mumkin.|They cannot have finished already.~Ular allaqachon tugatgan bo‘lishi mumkin emas.
117|Needn't have commonly implies an action occurred unnecessarily; didn't need to alone does not establish whether it occurred.|You should have called earlier.~Oldinroq qo‘ng‘iroq qilishingiz kerak edi.|I need not have hurried.~Shoshilishim shart emas ekan; ammo shoshildim.
118|Could have can express past possibility or opportunity; would have commonly depends on a condition or inferred outcome.|She could have won.~U yutishi mumkin edi.|I would have helped if you had asked.~So‘raganingizda yordam bergan bo‘lardim.
119|Modal, perfect and progressive auxiliaries combine in a fixed order, each adding its own meaning.|He must be sleeping.~U uxlayotgan bo‘lsa kerak.|They might have been working late.~Ular kechgacha ishlayotgan bo‘lgan bo‘lishi mumkin.
120|Some double-object verbs support passives focused on either recipient or thing; the pattern is not available for every verb.|She was given a certificate.~Unga sertifikat berildi.|A certificate was given to her.~Sertifikat unga berildi.
121|Reporting passives attribute a proposition or expectation; the wording does not itself establish that the proposition is true.|It is believed that the bridge is safe.~Ko‘prik xavfsiz deb hisoblanadi; bu qarash bildirilyapti.|The bridge is believed to be safe.~Ko‘prik xavfsiz deb hisoblanadi.
