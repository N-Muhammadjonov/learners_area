# Material deletion and Reading progress

Delete is separate from the reversible publication archive. It atomically creates an indexed deletion tombstone, withdraws publication and removes the material and its revisions from management/course catalogues. Source records are retained solely to preserve existing attempts, original question links, grades, evaluations and saved vocabulary. No learner results or shared storage objects are cascaded away. Deleted material cannot be restored through publication controls. Owner/admin and existing creator permissions remain enforced.

Delete controls cover TED/Podcast videos, Reading passages, Listening tests, Speaking materials/topics and Writing prompts/lessons/translations/model texts/patterns. The shared Materials editor and content-version workspace expose the action as well as publication lists.

Deleting an imported Reading set also deletes its member passages from catalogues. Tombstones remain authoritative even if a stale import republishes a source row. Direct starts and General progress reject deleted material; historical attempt detail continues to load the retained source rows. The database regression covers both a passage and its entire set, including stale publication and unchanged learner histories.

General Reading records opening as In progress without clearing saved answers or checked results. Answer edits save a draft; explicit Check marks Completed. Reopening a completed passage retains the result, and other learners remain independent. Pending drafts are flushed when leaving the reader. Redundant MCQ DAY chips and Reading CEFR badges are removed.

Validation: real PostgreSQL-compatible tests compare persisted Reading, Listening and video histories byte-for-byte across deletion, test ownership denial and rollback, catalogue exclusion, relational query aliases and idempotence. Another database test proves Not started -> In progress -> Completed -> reopen and user separation. Fourteen reader UI cases pass, including debounce, stale Check responses, restored feedback, contextual phrases and Academic restrictions. Production build and type/lint checks pass; updated catalogue/header tests retain current single-page product requirements.
